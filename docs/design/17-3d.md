# 17. 3D

Modern apps go beyond flat 2D: cards that flip in perspective, depth parallax, product
previews, avatars, 3D icons, scenes in themes. This is the plan for 3D in RemedyUI, in three
tiers. Tier 1 ships; tiers 2 and 3 are designed here and built as their own changes.

## Tier 1: 3D in effects (shipped)

Prism ([16](16-effects.md)) draws 3D procedurally, inside an element: cameras
(`look_at`, `camera_ray`, `project`), signed-distance shapes and operators, raymarching with
fixed step counts, normals, soft shadows, ambient occlusion, GGX shading, fog, volumes.
Examples: `orb.rfx`, `crystal.rfx`, `volumetric_clouds.rfx`. Safe for strangers and on both
renderers; costs are bounded at compile time.

Good for: decorative 3D, hero art, theme centrepieces. Not for: meshes, models, interaction
with 3D objects, 3D placement of UI.

## Tier 2: 3D transforms of elements (designed)

Elements placed in 3D: a card flip, a cover flow, a tilted panel, depth parallax.

- **Model.** `Layer.transform` becomes a projective 3×3 (2D homogeneous) for the composite,
  with a 4×4 kept for authoring: `El::transform_3d(Mat4)` and `El::perspective(distance,
  origin)` on a parent, as CSS `transform` and `perspective`. Children of a 3D-transformed
  parent flatten into its plane (CSS `transform-style: flat`); `preserve-3d` is not supported
  (no depth sorting between elements, which keeps hit testing and accessibility exact).
- **Raster.** A 3D-transformed layer is rastered flat at a scale chosen from its projected
  size (the largest local scale across the quad), then composited as a textured quad with a
  projective transform. Both compositors (vello_cpu and vello_gpu) draw images through affine
  transforms only, so the composite of a projective layer is a quad mesh: the layer split into
  a grid of affine triangles (8 × 8 cells is visually exact for a card), each drawn as a
  clipped, affine-mapped image. No new renderer feature is needed, and the CPU and GPU paths
  stay the same code.
- **Damage and clips.** Damage is the projected quad's bounding box. Clips inside the layer
  apply before projection (in the layer's plane); a projective clip of children is the
  projected quad.
- **Input.** Hit testing inverts the projection (a 3×3 inverse) to bring the pointer into
  the layer's plane; points behind the eye miss. Accessibility reports the projected bounding
  box.
- **Animation.** `animate_transform_3d` on the compositor, sampled per frame like opacity and
  offset today; reduced motion jumps to the end.
- **Cost.** One flat raster per content change plus 128 triangles a frame per moving layer;
  no new shaders.

## Tier 3: a 3D scene element (designed)

Meshes, materials and lights: product previews, avatars, 3D icons, scenes.

- **Element.** `scene_3d(ui, Scene) -> (El, SceneView)` in a new crate
  `remedy-ui-media-3d` (layer 5), built like `effect_view`: renders into textures the element
  owns, placed with `Item::Texture`; on the CPU renderer, a CPU rasterizer draws it.
- **Content.** glTF 2.0 (meshes, PBR metallic-roughness materials, textures, node hierarchy,
  skins and morph targets later), loaded off the UI thread with sizes checked against limits
  (vertices, triangles, texture pixels, nodes), like images are bounded in
  [08](08-security-and-privacy.md). Scenes are data: untrusted packages may ship models,
  never shaders.
- **GPU renderer.** A forward renderer with a fixed pipeline set made at start-up: PBR (GGX,
  image-based lighting from a small prefiltered environment), one shadow-casting directional
  light with a PCF shadow map, up to 8 point/spot lights, MSAA 4×, depth buffer, tone mapping.
  Prism effects can be a scene's background or a material's emissive map.
- **CPU path.** A tile-based triangle rasterizer (barycentric, perspective-correct,
  depth-buffered) at reduced resolution with the same material model minus shadows, or a
  still frame from the GPU when one exists. Parity tests as for effects.
- **Interaction.** Orbit, pan and zoom controllers; picking by ray against the scene's
  bounding volume hierarchy (CPU), so hit results are exact on both renderers.
- **Budgets.** Like effects: frames at the ambient rate when animated decoratively, at the
  display's rate while the user drags; still frame when paused or under reduced motion;
  triangle and pixel budgets measured on the reference machines.

## Order

1. Tier 2 (3D transforms): small, every app benefits, no new renderer features.
2. Tier 3 (scene element): its own crate; starts with static glTF + PBR + one light on the
   GPU, then the CPU rasterizer, then animation.
