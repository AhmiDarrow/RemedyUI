# Languages, scripts and directions

RemedyUI apps speak the world's most-used languages out of the box: English, Spanish, Simplified Chinese, Hindi, Arabic, French, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Japanese, German and Bengali. This page says what the engine does for each of them, how an app uses it, and how it is tested.

| Locale | Language | Script | Direction | Bundled face | Plural forms (cardinal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `en` | English | Latin | left to right | Remedy Sans UI | one, other |
| `es` | Spanish | Latin | left to right | Remedy Sans UI | one, many, other |
| `zh-Hans` | Chinese (Simplified) | Han | left to right | Noto Sans SC (on demand) | other |
| `hi` | Hindi | Devanagari | left to right | Noto Sans Devanagari | one, other |
| `ar` | Arabic | Arabic | **right to left** | Noto Sans Arabic | zero, one, two, few, many, other |
| `fr` | French | Latin | left to right | Remedy Sans UI | one, many, other |
| `pt-BR` | Portuguese (Brazil) | Latin | left to right | Remedy Sans UI | one, many, other |
| `ru` | Russian | Cyrillic | left to right | Remedy Sans UI | one, few, many, other |
| `ja` | Japanese | Han, kana | left to right | Noto Sans JP (on demand) | other |
| `de` | German | Latin | left to right | Remedy Sans UI | one, other |
| `bn` | Bengali | Bengali | left to right | Noto Sans Bengali | one, other |

A locale the engine does not ship falls back to the closest one it does (`pt-PT` to `pt-BR`, `zh-SG` to `zh-Hans`, `es-MX` to `es`), then to English.

## Direction

A window lays itself out from its locale: right to left for Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Urdu and the other right-to-left languages (a script subtag decides when there is one: `pa-Arab` is right to left, `ug-Latn` is not). Everything inside follows:

- **Layout.** Rows run from the start edge; padding, margins, insets, alignment and absolute positions are logical (`start`/`end`), so a right-to-left window is the mirror image with no code of its own. Overlays (menus, dialogs, toasts, tooltips) inherit the window's direction.
- **Icons.** Icons that point along the reading direction mirror on their own (`Icon::mirrors_in_rtl`): back and forward arrows, chevrons (a "next" chevron points left in Arabic), send, open-outside, quotes, and lists, whose bullets, numbers and boxes move to the right without being drawn backwards. Icons of things in the world (clocks, padlocks, play and pause, search, check marks) do not mirror. An icon that must point a physical way sets its element's direction to left to right.
- **Controls.** Sliders, progress bars, switches and split panes mirror; their arrow keys move the way they point (Left increases a right-to-left slider). Steppers, carousels, tabs, step flows and date pickers follow their row.
- **Scrolling.** A sideways-scrolling container in a right-to-left window opens at the start of its content (its right edge), scrolls left to reach the rest, and stays anchored to the start as its content grows (`Layer::scroll_origin`). The page viewer puts its vertical scroll bar at the left edge.
- **Not mirrored.** Music notation, fretboards and chord boxes read left to right in every language, as musicians read them; timelines and graphs keep their own direction.

A person can choose: `App::set_direction(WindowDirection::RightToLeft)` (or `LeftToRight`, or back to `Locale`) for every window, `Ui::set_direction` for one. `Ui::is_rtl` is tracked, and painters read `PaintCx::rtl`.

```rust
use remedy_ui::{App, WindowDirection};
fn on_setting_changed(app: &App, keep_left_to_right: bool) {
    app.set_direction(if keep_left_to_right { WindowDirection::LeftToRight } else { WindowDirection::Locale });
}
```

## Text

Shaping covers every script above, including Arabic joining, Devanagari and Bengali conjuncts and reordering, and kana and kanji. Paragraphs run the Unicode bidirectional algorithm: an English name inside an Arabic sentence (and the reverse) keeps its order, and placeholders filled into messages are wrapped in isolation marks so a value never scrambles the sentence around it.

- **Language.** A window's text engine knows the window's language (`TextEngine::set_locale`), which picks between fonts where languages share characters: Japanese and Chinese draw 直, 骨 and 写 each in their own forms.
- **Editing.** In text fields, Left and Right move the caret the way the text is drawn: in Arabic, Right moves toward the start; through mixed lines the caret steps across each run in drawn order and never jumps a run. Ctrl (Option on Apple platforms) with an arrow moves by words the same way, Shift extends the selection, and a selection collapses to the edge the arrow points at. Where one index has two places on screen (the end of an English word and the start of the Arabic word after it), the field remembers which side the caret is on. Selections across a direction change are drawn in as many pieces as the text is. Input methods (Chinese pinyin, Japanese kana-kanji, Hindi and Bengali transliteration) compose inline and commit as one undo step.
- **Line breaking** follows UAX #14 for every script; Chinese and Japanese break between characters. Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese need the `complex-scripts` feature for dictionary breaking.

## Fonts

Remedy Sans UI writes Latin (every letter Spanish, French, Portuguese and German need) and Cyrillic. The `script-fonts` feature (on by default) bundles Noto faces for the rest, so nothing shows as empty boxes even where no such font is installed: the web, a minimal Linux, a CI machine.

| Face | Crate | Size | Loaded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noto Sans Arabic, Devanagari, Bengali (Regular, Bold) | `remedy-ui-fonts` (`scripts`) | 0.9 MB | with the window |
| Noto Sans SC, Noto Sans JP (Regular, Bold) | `remedy-ui-fonts-cjk` | 8.3 MB (WOFF2) | the first time text needs them; ahead of time on a background thread when the app starts in Chinese or Japanese |

`remedy_ui::bundled_fonts()` is the collection every window starts with: the bundled faces, each script face registered as its script's fallback (`FontCollection::add_script_fallback`) ahead of the system's, the Chinese and Japanese faces on demand (`FontCollection::add_on_demand`), and the system's fonts after them with `system-fonts`. An app with its own brand face adds it and keeps the fallbacks. An app that will never show these scripts can turn `script-fonts` off and save the space.

The Chinese face covers every hanzi of GB 2312 and some 6,000 more; the Japanese face every kanji of JIS X 0208 and more. Rarer characters (uncommon names, historic forms) come from the system's fonts.

## The engine's own words

Every built-in widget's words — button text, menus, placeholders, empty states, keyboard hints and what screen readers say — are Fluent messages in each crate's `i18n/<locale>.ftl`, compiled in as that crate's `STRINGS` catalog (`remedy_ui_view::strings`). A widget asks for a message in its window's locale and shows it again when the locale changes. Plurals follow each language's rules (Arabic has six forms, Russian four), numbers are written the locale's way (Arabic-Indic digits in Arabic, `1.234,5` in German), and placeholders are isolated for mixed directions.

Apps reword a built-in message, or add a language the engine does not ship, with `add_messages`:

```rust
// Turkish for the core widgets, and the app's own word for one message.
remedy_ui::view::strings::add_messages("widgets-core", "tr", include_str!("../i18n/engine/widgets-core.tr.ftl"));
remedy_ui::view::strings::add_messages("widgets-core", "en", "prompt-allow = Let it");
```

The app's own words use `remedy_ui::i18n::Messages` (Fluent, with user overrides and live reload) or `Catalog` (keyed by source text); dates, times and numbers use `CalendarSymbols` and `NumberSymbols` from ICU data.

### Adding a language to the engine

1. Add its tag to `LOCALES` in `remedy-ui-view/src/strings.rs` and to the `engine_strings!` macro.
2. Copy each crate's `i18n/en.ftl` to `i18n/<tag>.ftl` and translate it. Keep every `{ $placeholder }`; give every plural form the language has.
3. Run `cargo test --workspace`: each crate's catalog test (`strings::check`) lists what is missing, what does not parse, placeholders that differ from English, and plural forms the language needs.
4. If the script is new, add its face to `remedy-ui-fonts` (or a crate of its own if large) and register it in `script_fallbacks`.

## Testing

- **Catalogs.** Every crate with words tests `strings::check(&STRINGS)`: all eleven locales present and parsed, every message in every locale, the same placeholders as English, every CLDR plural category, nothing left untranslated, everything formatting.
- **Fonts.** `remedy-ui-text/tests/scripts.rs` shapes a line in each language (and a line mixing seven scripts) at Regular and Bold with the bundled faces alone and finds no missing glyph, each script in its own face, Japanese and Chinese in their own forms.
- **Bidi editing.** `remedy-ui-view/tests/bidi.rs` walks the caret through Arabic, English-with-Arabic and Arabic-with-English lines, by character and word, with selections, in plain and rich fields; `remedy-ui-text/tests/bidi.rs` checks selection rectangles, hit testing and caret places at direction changes.
- **Direction.** `remedy-ui-view/tests/direction.rs` (locale, override, overlays, sideways scrolling), the widgets' mirroring tests, and the page viewer's scroll bar.
- **Golden images.** `remedy-ui-devtools/tests/languages.rs` renders the same settings screen in all eleven languages and mixed-direction paragraphs in both paragraph directions on the CPU renderer, against `tests/golden/languages-*.png`. Run with `REMEDY_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` after a deliberate change, and look at every image.
- **Pseudo-locales.** `en-XA` (accented and longer) and `ar-XB` (right to left) rewrite the app's own text to check layouts before translations exist.
